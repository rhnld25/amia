# Graham: price versus value, with a margin of safety

- A stock is a share of a business, not a ticker. Judge it by what the business earns and owns, not by recent price action.
- Intrinsic value is an estimate with a range, never a point. Buy only when the price sits well below the low end of that range; the gap (margin of safety) absorbs errors in the estimate and bad luck.
- Prefer long, unbroken records: profits every year for a decade, dividends paid without interruption, earnings that grew over the period. A long record is evidence; a forecast is a hope.
- Balance-sheet strength matters for a defensive investor: current assets comfortably above current liabilities, and long-term debt no larger than net working capital.
- Pay a moderate multiple. Very high price-to-earnings or price-to-book ratios assume a future that may not arrive.
- Mr. Market offers a price every day and is often emotional. His mood is an opportunity, never an instruction. Sharp drops in sound businesses can be chances to buy; euphoric prices are chances to sell or wait.
- When the screen fails, ask why. A strong business can fail a criterion for a benign reason (no dividend because it reinvests at high returns), and a weak one can pass by accident. The criteria inform judgment; they do not replace it.
- Graham's formula undervalues fast, durable growers and overvalues cyclicals at peak earnings. Weigh it with the business's quality and the cycle.
