# Marks: cycles, risk and second-level thinking

- Markets swing like a pendulum between greed and fear, rarely resting at the fair midpoint. The biggest risks arise when optimism is widespread and prices assume everything will go right; the best opportunities arise when fear is widespread.
- Risk is mostly the chance of permanent loss, not volatility. It is highest when it feels lowest: when credit is easy, spreads are tight, and caution is dismissed.
- Second-level thinking: the question is not whether news is good, but whether it is better or worse than what the price already reflects. Good news on a heavily owned, euphoric stock can still disappoint; bad news on a despised stock can already be priced in.
- Read the temperature: tight credit spreads, strong recent index gains and enthusiastic coverage suggest a hot market that calls for more caution and higher standards. Wide spreads, deep drawdowns and gloomy coverage suggest a cold market where patient buyers are paid.
- Know where the stock sits in its own cycle: near a 52-week high after a strong run with heavy, upbeat attention is a greed reading; deep below its high with uniformly negative coverage but intact fundamentals is a fear reading that deserves a closer look.
- Attention itself is information. A sudden surge in coverage often marks the late stage of a move. Ask whether the narrative is new and material, or just loud.
- We cannot predict the future, but we can know where we stand. Adjust aggressiveness to the cycle rather than forecasting turning points.
- Avoiding losers matters more than catching every winner. When the case rests on things going right, demand a larger margin of safety or pass.
